Section 1: Equipment Overview

An equipment file is kept at the control room containing the as-built drawings, previous maintenance records, and the modification log for the DP-310.

The DP-310 is installed on a rigid fabricated skid with anchoring bolts and isolation where specified. Adjacent clearances must allow safe access for inspection and removal of the guard or cover.

Plant personnel interact with the DP-310 mainly during routine rounds, preventive maintenance, and corrective interventions. This document standardizes those activities.

The DP-310 Metering Dosing Pump injects treatment chemical into the cooling water circuit at a precisely controlled stroke rate. It is designed for continuous duty in a ventilated industrial environment and is rated for 0.75 kW at its nominal operating point.

The drive train consists of a prime mover, a coupling or transmission element, and the working element of the machine. Each element contributes stored energy that must be controlled before work begins.

Operators record running hours, abnormal noise, leakage, and vibration observations in the shift log. These records form the basis for the diagnostic steps in Section 2.

Section 3: Safety Prerequisites

1. Use calibrated lifting equipment for components above 25 kg and follow rigging plan for heavy lifts.
2. Barricade the work area and post a sign when guards or safety devices are removed.
3. A second qualified person must be present for work involving chemical under pressure or diaphragm rupture.

5. Confirm the working element and shaft are completely stationary before reaching through any opening or removing any guard.
6. Isolate all energy sources feeding the DP-310 and apply lockout/tagout per site procedure before accessing the equipment.

Section 4: Diagnostic Procedure

1. Feel-check accessible bearing housings for abnormal temperature after a minimum of 30 minutes of operation.
2. Measure motor current on all phases and compare with the rated value; a persistent imbalance above 5 percent requires investigation.
3. Check mounting hardware, foundation bolts, and hold-down clamps for looseness or fretting marks.
4. Verify that lubricant levels are within the sight glass marks and that lubricant color is normal.
5. Check instrumentation readings against calibrated test instruments where the installed gauge is suspected.
6. Listen for bearing noise, imbalance, rubbing, or irregular knocking while the machine is running under load.

Section 5: Maintenance Procedure

1. Clean the work area, remove tools and consumables, and dispose of waste lubricant and parts per site procedure.
2. Remove accumulated dust and debris from guards, housings, screens, and ventilation openings using low-pressure air or vacuum.
3. Re-torque foundation and coupling hardware to the values in the appendix after the first 50 hours of operation.
4. Examine bearings, bushings, and mounts for wear, looseness, or excessive movement; measure and record clearances.
5. Renew lubricant according to the schedule in Section 5, and verify the lubricant type against the specification table.
6. Replace worn seals, gaskets, and O-rings; pressure-test or leak-check the reassembled unit before return to service.
7. Reinstall guards and safety devices, confirm the area is clear, and perform a controlled restart while monitoring current, vibration, and temperature.

Section 6: Common Failure Modes

Looseness produces broadband vibration with harmonics and is often mistaken for bearing damage. Verify foundation and hold-down condition before condemning bearings.

Bearing-related failures account for the largest share of unplanned interventions on rotating equipment of this class. Contributing factors include contamination ingress, lubricant degradation, misalignment, and overload.

Electrical failures are frequently preceded by elevated current, hot connection points, or insulation odor. Infrared scans during normal operation are an effective screening tool.

Section 11: Lubrication Requirements

Lubrication intervals assume clean operating conditions and rated load. Halve the interval in dusty, hot, or humid environments.

Verify compatibility before mixing lubricants. When changing lubricant type, flush the cavity and renew seals.

Over-greasing is a common cause of seal failure and overheating. Apply the quantity specified in the table and purge old grease through the relief port.

Record every lubrication event with lubricant batch number in the CMMS to support warranty and failure analysis.

Section 12: Operating Limits and Precautions

Frequent starts and stops stress the drive train. Allow the minimum cooldown or run-up interval specified before re-energizing.

Operating the DP-310 above its rated point reduces component life exponentially; a 10 percent overload can halve bearing life.

Monitor the trend of the parameters in Section 2 rather than absolute alarms alone; slow drift gives early warning of developing faults.

Do not operate with guards removed, interlocks defeated, or known leaks unattended.

Section 13: General Workmanship Notes

Communication guidance: Hand over open findings to the next shift in writing, including the isolation status and any parts on order.

Measurement guidance: Record ambient conditions together with equipment readings so that comparisons across seasons remain meaningful.

Housekeeping guidance: Dust and debris are both a fire load and a heat-insulating layer. Keep the equipment surroundings clean as a standing condition, not a periodic task.

Quality guidance: Use only parts listed in the spare parts reference. Substitute parts require engineering approval before installation.

General guidance: Always begin an intervention with a review of the equipment history. The last three work orders often reveal a pattern that isolates the current fault faster than any single measurement.
