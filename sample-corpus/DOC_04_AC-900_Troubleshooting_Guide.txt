Section 1: Equipment Overview

Design ambient conditions are -10 °C to +40 °C at a maximum altitude of 1000 m. Derating is required outside this envelope.

Plant personnel interact with the AC-900 mainly during routine rounds, preventive maintenance, and corrective interventions. This document standardizes those activities.

An equipment file is kept at the control room containing the as-built drawings, previous maintenance records, and the modification log for the AC-900.

The AC-900 forms part of the Compressed Air group in the plant equipment register under asset tag AC-900-A. Its failure can interrupt the associated process train.

The unit operates at 3000 rpm under normal load conditions. Operating outside the envelope defined in Section 2 accelerates wear on bearings, seals, and drive components.

The equipment was selected for the duty point described in the process datasheet. Any change to flow, pressure, speed, or handled medium invalidates the assumptions in this document.

Section 3: Safety Prerequisites

1. A second qualified person must be present for work involving high pressure air or hot oil circuit.
2. Use calibrated lifting equipment for components above 25 kg and follow rigging plan for heavy lifts.
3. Barricade the work area and post a sign when guards or safety devices are removed.
4. Verify the absence of hazardous process media — pressure, temperature, chemicals — before opening any cover or flange.
5. Confirm fire watch and hot-work permit requirements before any grinding, welding, or flame cutting nearby.
6. Release stored mechanical, hydraulic, pneumatic, and electrical energy and verify zero energy state with a test-before-touch check.

Section 4: Diagnostic Procedure

1. Inspect the guards, covers, and openings for dust accumulation, foreign objects, or signs of rubbing contact.
2. Check instrumentation readings against calibrated test instruments where the installed gauge is suspected.
3. Inspect belts, couplings, and drive elements for glazing, cracking, misalignment, or abnormal wear patterns.
4. Inspect seals and gaskets for weepage, and confirm that drain and vent lines are clear.
5. Listen for bearing noise, imbalance, rubbing, or irregular knocking while the machine is running under load.
6. Verify that lubricant levels are within the sight glass marks and that lubricant color is normal.

Section 5: Maintenance Procedure

1. Replace worn seals, gaskets, and O-rings; pressure-test or leak-check the reassembled unit before return to service.
2. Record all findings, measurements, and replaced parts in the maintenance management system before closing the work order.
3. Examine bearings, bushings, and mounts for wear, looseness, or excessive movement; measure and record clearances.
4. Renew lubricant according to the schedule in Section 5, and verify the lubricant type against the specification table.
5. Remove accumulated dust and debris from guards, housings, screens, and ventilation openings using low-pressure air or vacuum.
6. Re-torque foundation and coupling hardware to the values in the appendix after the first 50 hours of operation.
7. Verify alignment between driver and driven elements using the specified tolerance limits.

Section 6: Common Failure Modes

Cavitation and flow recirculation damage working elements progressively. A crackling noise resembling gravel indicates cavitation risk and requires immediate review of operating conditions.

Imbalance develops from material buildup, erosion, or loss of rotating mass. Vibration spectra showing a dominant 1× running speed component point to imbalance or misalignment.

Electrical failures are frequently preceded by elevated current, hot connection points, or insulation odor. Infrared scans during normal operation are an effective screening tool.

Section 7: Troubleshooting Guide

| Symptom | Probable Causes | Recommended Action |
|---|---|---|
| Excessive vibration | Loose mounting hardware, imbalance, misalignment, bearing wear | Re-torque mounts; check balance and alignment; inspect bearings |

| Erratic instrument readings | Sensor drift, wiring fault, moisture ingress | Calibrate sensor; inspect wiring and junctions |

| Pressure / flow instability | Cavitation risk, control instability, partial blockage | Verify suction conditions; tune controls; inspect passages |
| Current above rating | Mechanical overload, voltage imbalance, phase loss, driven element binding | Measure phases; inspect driven element; check supply quality |

Section 11: Lubrication Requirements

Lubrication intervals assume clean operating conditions and rated load. Halve the interval in dusty, hot, or humid environments.

Verify compatibility before mixing lubricants. When changing lubricant type, flush the cavity and renew seals.

Over-greasing is a common cause of seal failure and overheating. Apply the quantity specified in the table and purge old grease through the relief port.

Record every lubrication event with lubricant batch number in the CMMS to support warranty and failure analysis.

Section 12: Operating Limits and Precautions

Do not operate with guards removed, interlocks defeated, or known leaks unattended.

Monitor the trend of the parameters in Section 2 rather than absolute alarms alone; slow drift gives early warning of developing faults.

Operating the AC-900 above its rated point reduces component life exponentially; a 10 percent overload can halve bearing life.

Frequent starts and stops stress the drive train. Allow the minimum cooldown or run-up interval specified before re-energizing.

Section 13: General Workmanship Notes

Measurement guidance: Record ambient conditions together with equipment readings so that comparisons across seasons remain meaningful.

Quality guidance: Use only parts listed in the spare parts reference. Substitute parts require engineering approval before installation.

Communication guidance: Hand over open findings to the next shift in writing, including the isolation status and any parts on order.

General guidance: Always begin an intervention with a review of the equipment history. The last three work orders often reveal a pattern that isolates the current fault faster than any single measurement.

Housekeeping guidance: Dust and debris are both a fire load and a heat-insulating layer. Keep the equipment surroundings clean as a standing condition, not a periodic task.

Appendix C: Synthetic Case Studies

Case study (synthetic): A similar unit in the corpus fleet showed rising 1X vibration over six weeks. Investigation found foundation bolt looseness from thermal cycling after a process upset. Re-torquing to specification restored baseline vibration without part replacement.

Case study (synthetic): Recurring seal leaks were found to follow a lubricant change. The replacement lubricant was incompatible with the elastomer, causing swelling. Restoring the specified lubricant and renewing seals eliminated the leaks.

Case study (synthetic): A unit tripped repeatedly on overload. The driven element was binding after a repair left a spacer mispositioned. Correct reassembly and a controlled restart confirmed normal current draw.
