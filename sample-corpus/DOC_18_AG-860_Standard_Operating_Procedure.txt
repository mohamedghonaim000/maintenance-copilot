Section 1: Equipment Overview

Design ambient conditions are -10 °C to +40 °C at a maximum altitude of 1000 m. Derating is required outside this envelope.

The AG-860 Agitator Assembly blends the vessel contents to maintain suspension uniformity in the blending tank. It is designed for continuous duty in a ventilated industrial environment and is rated for 11 kW at its nominal operating point.

Plant personnel interact with the AG-860 mainly during routine rounds, preventive maintenance, and corrective interventions. This document standardizes those activities.

The drive train consists of a prime mover, a coupling or transmission element, and the working element of the machine. Each element contributes stored energy that must be controlled before work begins.

The unit operates at 85 rpm under normal load conditions. Operating outside the envelope defined in Section 2 accelerates wear on bearings, seals, and drive components.

The AG-860 forms part of the Mixing group in the plant equipment register under asset tag AG-860-A. Its failure can interrupt the associated process train.

Section 3: Safety Prerequisites

1. Never bypass, jumper, or defeat an interlock, guard switch, or protective device.
2. Use calibrated lifting equipment for components above 25 kg and follow rigging plan for heavy lifts.
3. Wear safety glasses, hearing protection, gloves, and safety shoes while working in the vicinity of the equipment.
4. Barricade the work area and post a sign when guards or safety devices are removed.
5. A second qualified person must be present for work involving rotating impeller or vessel contents.
6. Confirm the working element and shaft are completely stationary before reaching through any opening or removing any guard.

Section 4: Diagnostic Procedure

1. Listen for bearing noise, imbalance, rubbing, or irregular knocking while the machine is running under load.
2. Review the shift log and trend data for drifting parameters that preceded the current condition.
3. Inspect the guards, covers, and openings for dust accumulation, foreign objects, or signs of rubbing contact.
4. Check instrumentation readings against calibrated test instruments where the installed gauge is suspected.
5. Check mounting hardware, foundation bolts, and hold-down clamps for looseness or fretting marks.
6. Feel-check accessible bearing housings for abnormal temperature after a minimum of 30 minutes of operation.

Section 5: Maintenance Procedure

1. Reinstall guards and safety devices, confirm the area is clear, and perform a controlled restart while monitoring current, vibration, and temperature.
2. Inspect the working element for cracks, deformation, imbalance, or foreign-object damage; replace damaged components.
3. Function-test interlocks, sensors, and protective devices after any intervention that disturbs them.
4. Clean the work area, remove tools and consumables, and dispose of waste lubricant and parts per site procedure.
5. Remove accumulated dust and debris from guards, housings, screens, and ventilation openings using low-pressure air or vacuum.
6. Isolate electrical power, lock out the disconnect, and tag the isolation point before any physical work.
7. Renew lubricant according to the schedule in Section 5, and verify the lubricant type against the specification table.

Section 6: Common Failure Modes

Cavitation and flow recirculation damage working elements progressively. A crackling noise resembling gravel indicates cavitation risk and requires immediate review of operating conditions.

Imbalance develops from material buildup, erosion, or loss of rotating mass. Vibration spectra showing a dominant 1× running speed component point to imbalance or misalignment.

Bearing-related failures account for the largest share of unplanned interventions on rotating equipment of this class. Contributing factors include contamination ingress, lubricant degradation, misalignment, and overload.

Section 11: Lubrication Requirements

Record every lubrication event with lubricant batch number in the CMMS to support warranty and failure analysis.

Verify compatibility before mixing lubricants. When changing lubricant type, flush the cavity and renew seals.

Over-greasing is a common cause of seal failure and overheating. Apply the quantity specified in the table and purge old grease through the relief port.

Lubrication intervals assume clean operating conditions and rated load. Halve the interval in dusty, hot, or humid environments.

Section 12: Operating Limits and Precautions

Do not operate with guards removed, interlocks defeated, or known leaks unattended.

Frequent starts and stops stress the drive train. Allow the minimum cooldown or run-up interval specified before re-energizing.

Monitor the trend of the parameters in Section 2 rather than absolute alarms alone; slow drift gives early warning of developing faults.

Operating the AG-860 above its rated point reduces component life exponentially; a 10 percent overload can halve bearing life.

Section 13: General Workmanship Notes

Housekeeping guidance: Dust and debris are both a fire load and a heat-insulating layer. Keep the equipment surroundings clean as a standing condition, not a periodic task.

Measurement guidance: Record ambient conditions together with equipment readings so that comparisons across seasons remain meaningful.

Quality guidance: Use only parts listed in the spare parts reference. Substitute parts require engineering approval before installation.

General guidance: Always begin an intervention with a review of the equipment history. The last three work orders often reveal a pattern that isolates the current fault faster than any single measurement.

Communication guidance: Hand over open findings to the next shift in writing, including the isolation status and any parts on order.
