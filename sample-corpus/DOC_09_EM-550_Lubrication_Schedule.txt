Section 1: Equipment Overview

The unit operates at 1475 rpm under normal load conditions. Operating outside the envelope defined in Section 2 accelerates wear on bearings, seals, and drive components.

The EM-550 is installed on a rigid fabricated skid with anchoring bolts and isolation where specified. Adjacent clearances must allow safe access for inspection and removal of the guard or cover.

Design ambient conditions are -10 °C to +40 °C at a maximum altitude of 1000 m. Derating is required outside this envelope.

An equipment file is kept at the control room containing the as-built drawings, previous maintenance records, and the modification log for the EM-550.

Plant personnel interact with the EM-550 mainly during routine rounds, preventive maintenance, and corrective interventions. This document standardizes those activities.

The EM-550 forms part of the Electrical group in the plant equipment register under asset tag EM-550-A. Its failure can interrupt the associated process train.

Section 3: Safety Prerequisites

1. Never bypass, jumper, or defeat an interlock, guard switch, or protective device.
2. Confirm fire watch and hot-work permit requirements before any grinding, welding, or flame cutting nearby.
3. Confirm the working element and shaft are completely stationary before reaching through any opening or removing any guard.
4. Release stored mechanical, hydraulic, pneumatic, and electrical energy and verify zero energy state with a test-before-touch check.
5. Isolate all energy sources feeding the EM-550 and apply lockout/tagout per site procedure before accessing the equipment.
6. A second qualified person must be present for work involving live terminals or rotating shaft.

Section 4: Diagnostic Procedure

1. Review the shift log and trend data for drifting parameters that preceded the current condition.
2. Check instrumentation readings against calibrated test instruments where the installed gauge is suspected.
3. Measure motor current on all phases and compare with the rated value; a persistent imbalance above 5 percent requires investigation.
4. Inspect belts, couplings, and drive elements for glazing, cracking, misalignment, or abnormal wear patterns.
5. Inspect seals and gaskets for weepage, and confirm that drain and vent lines are clear.
6. Verify that lubricant levels are within the sight glass marks and that lubricant color is normal.

Section 5: Maintenance Procedure

1. Examine bearings, bushings, and mounts for wear, looseness, or excessive movement; measure and record clearances.
2. Reinstall guards and safety devices, confirm the area is clear, and perform a controlled restart while monitoring current, vibration, and temperature.
3. Verify alignment between driver and driven elements using the specified tolerance limits.
4. Record all findings, measurements, and replaced parts in the maintenance management system before closing the work order.
5. Inspect the working element for cracks, deformation, imbalance, or foreign-object damage; replace damaged components.
6. Function-test interlocks, sensors, and protective devices after any intervention that disturbs them.
7. Remove accumulated dust and debris from guards, housings, screens, and ventilation openings using low-pressure air or vacuum.

Section 6: Common Failure Modes

Seal and gasket leaks rarely develop suddenly. Trend weepage during rounds; a stain that doubles between checks indicates an active leak path that will progress.

Bearing-related failures account for the largest share of unplanned interventions on rotating equipment of this class. Contributing factors include contamination ingress, lubricant degradation, misalignment, and overload.

Imbalance develops from material buildup, erosion, or loss of rotating mass. Vibration spectra showing a dominant 1× running speed component point to imbalance or misalignment.

Section 7: Troubleshooting Guide

| Symptom | Probable Causes | Recommended Action |
|---|---|---|
| Excessive vibration | Loose mounting hardware, imbalance, misalignment, bearing wear | Re-torque mounts; check balance and alignment; inspect bearings |
| Unusual odor or smoke | Insulation overheating, lubricant breakdown, friction contact | Stop immediately; investigate before restart |
| Reduced output / performance | Worn working element, internal leakage, blocked passage, speed drift | Inspect element; check internal clearances; clean passages |
| Overheating | Overloading, blocked ventilation, insufficient lubricant, high ambient | Reduce load; clean openings; check lubricant; verify ambient |
| Current above rating | Mechanical overload, voltage imbalance, phase loss, driven element binding | Measure phases; inspect driven element; check supply quality |
| Slow or failed start | Low supply voltage, seized working element, control fault | Measure starting voltage; rotate element by hand after isolation |
| Frequent protective trips | Relay settings, genuine overload, instrumentation fault | Review trip records; test relay; verify instrument calibration |
| Leakage at seals or joints | Worn seals, loose fasteners, gasket damage, thermal cycling | Replace seals; re-torque to spec; renew gaskets |

Section 11: Lubrication Requirements

Over-greasing is a common cause of seal failure and overheating. Apply the quantity specified in the table and purge old grease through the relief port.

Record every lubrication event with lubricant batch number in the CMMS to support warranty and failure analysis.

Verify compatibility before mixing lubricants. When changing lubricant type, flush the cavity and renew seals.

Lubrication intervals assume clean operating conditions and rated load. Halve the interval in dusty, hot, or humid environments.

Section 12: Operating Limits and Precautions

Do not operate with guards removed, interlocks defeated, or known leaks unattended.

Monitor the trend of the parameters in Section 2 rather than absolute alarms alone; slow drift gives early warning of developing faults.

Frequent starts and stops stress the drive train. Allow the minimum cooldown or run-up interval specified before re-energizing.

Operating the EM-550 above its rated point reduces component life exponentially; a 10 percent overload can halve bearing life.

Section 13: General Workmanship Notes

General guidance: Always begin an intervention with a review of the equipment history. The last three work orders often reveal a pattern that isolates the current fault faster than any single measurement.

Measurement guidance: Record ambient conditions together with equipment readings so that comparisons across seasons remain meaningful.

Quality guidance: Use only parts listed in the spare parts reference. Substitute parts require engineering approval before installation.

Communication guidance: Hand over open findings to the next shift in writing, including the isolation status and any parts on order.

Housekeeping guidance: Dust and debris are both a fire load and a heat-insulating layer. Keep the equipment surroundings clean as a standing condition, not a periodic task.
